# An unconditional infinite rank-one family with zero second target

## Statement

There are infinitely many rational a in (-1,3), giving infinitely many geometric isomorphism classes of C_a: y²=x⁶+a x⁴+a x²+1, such that:

1. E_a(Q) has rank one and J_a(Q) has rank two.
2. a belongs to Z_2 and a=3 modulo 8.
3. Every positive odd valuation of a+1 is exactly one.
4. For every good odd prime p, every away-from-p actual local image of the second height is {0}, hence Omega*={0}.

For any fixed odd prime p and N>=1, the infinite family can moreover be chosen with v_p(a+5)>=N; then p is good. These are rational parameters. Since (-1,3) contains only finitely many integers, no infinitude of integral parameters is claimed.

This strengthens the prior rational-rank infinitude result by proving the second-target condition for an infinite subfamily. It does not prove first-target vanishing, the integral-parameter global height formula, or a full depth-two equality.

## Imported existence theorem and local data

We use D. Zywina, [Rank one elliptic curves and rank stability, arXiv:2505.16960v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16960v1), Theorem 1.5 and its proof in Sections 3–4. Rename his parameters m,b. Over Q the construction produces S-integers with 0<m<b, rank-one curve

    F: y²=x³+4m(m+b)x²+2m(m+b)²(m-b)x,

and infinitely many distinct j(F). The local valuation triples (v(m),v(m+b),v(m-b)) are:

    S0: (0,0,0)
    S1: (0,1,0), (-1,1,-1), (0,2,0), or (1,0,0)
    S2: (-1,1,-1)
    S3: (0,0,1).

The last S1 option is the auxiliary S1' absorbed in Lemma 3.1. Outside S, m,m+b,m-b generate distinct prime ideals of the S-integer ring, each with exponent one, as in (3.13). Theorem 1.5, Lemma 4.1 and the shrinking-open argument in Section 4 supply existence and infinitude with these conditions.

## Transfer to the reciprocal family

Put u=m+b and

    a=(3b-5m)/(b+m),  A=a-3=-8m/u,
    a+1=4(b-m)/u,     a+5=8b/u.

Then -1<a<3. The change X_F'=u²X, Y_F'=u³Y sends E_a to

    F': Y²=X³-8mu X²+8mu³X,

the 2-isogenous partner of F. Hence E_a has rank one. The second quotient E'_a: Y²=Z³-2AZ²+A(A+4)Z maps to F by X_F=(u/2)²Z, Y_F=(u/2)³Y. The (2,2)-isogeny decomposition of J_a therefore gives rank two.

## Valuations of a+1

For odd q,

    v_q(a+1)=v_q(m-b)-v_q(m+b).

Applying this difference to the displayed triples gives respectively:

    S0: 0
    S1: -1,-2,-2,0
    S2: -2
    S3: +1.

Outside S it gives -1 at the prime generated by m+b, +1 at the prime generated by m-b, and zero elsewhere. Thus every positive odd valuation is exactly one. This conclusion uses the exponent-one prime-ideal identities, not merely prime support.

At 2, all three elements m,m+b,m-b are units, so m is odd and b even. Therefore

    v_2(a-3)=3,  v_2(a+1)=2,  a+5 in 16 Z_2.

In particular a=3 modulo 8.

## Actual second targets

At an odd q where a is integral, the local proofs of reciprocal_local_targets.md apply: they require local, not global, integrality. The target is zero when v_q(a+1)=0 or 1. At an odd denominator prime it is zero by rational_denominator_targets.md. At 2 the dyadic calculation applies because a=3 modulo 8.

These cases exhaust all finite primes other than p. Every local domain is nonempty, as the second model has rational points (u,v)=(±1,±4). Hence the actual images are {0}, not empty, and their sum is {0}.

## Distinct curves and a prescribed good prime

The second quotient has

    j(F)=64(5m+3b)³/[(m-b)²(m+b)].

The source's distinct-j construction preserves all the preceding local opens and therefore all their valuation properties. Distinct j(F) give infinitely many parameters. A fixed smooth genus-two curve in characteristic zero has only finitely many degree-two elliptic quotients, because its automorphism group is finite. Thus a subsequence consists of geometrically nonisomorphic C_a.

For the optional p,N assertion, include p in S0 at the beginning. This is permitted by the definition of S0 in Section 3. At p choose the allowed local center m_p=1 with nonzero b_p divisible by a sufficiently high power of p, and shrink the open neighborhood. Then a+5=8b/(m+b) has valuation at least N. Both a-3 and a+1 remain p-adic units, so C_a and its quotients have good reduction. Section 4 still supplies infinitely many distinct j-values. One is making a permitted initial choice, not adding an arbitrary condition to already fixed local opens.

## What is still missing

For rational a, the first local target at denominator primes need not contain zero, and h_p(P)=lambda_p(P)+(1/2)log_p(D), with D the positive reduced denominator. Imposing a+5 close to zero at p does not by itself eliminate this shift or prove the prior analytic height hypotheses. The non-Tate central conditions of full depth two also remain separate. The theorem does not assert that the full depth-two set consists only of rational points for infinitely many parameters.
