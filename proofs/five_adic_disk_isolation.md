# Six scalar zeros and four full depth-two points at p=5

Status: complete and independently reviewed. The exact tripling and primitive identities, the all-order bounds at five, endpoint and Weierstrass domination, disk exhaustion, and uniform period transfer have passed independent review. The nonzero residual period was separately certified by two independently written implementations. No assertion about the full set is deduced merely from scalar membership.

## 1. Statements and conventions

Let a be an odd integer divisible by 5, and put

    C_a: y²=x⁶+a x⁴+a x²+1,
    E_a: Y²=X³+AX²−AX,  A=a−3,  P=(1,1).

Use ω=dX/(2Y), the complement generated by (X−2)ω, and the compatible odd primitive and normalized local height

    dζ=−(X−2)ω,  dλ=−2ζω,
    ζ(t)=1/t+O(t),  λ(t)=−2log₅(t)−2B(t),  t=−X/Y.

Set ell=L(P), h=λ(P), Z=ζ(P), alpha=h/ell². Define

    f₁=(1+x²,y),  f₂=(1+x⁻²,y/x³),
    rho=λ(f₁)−λ(f₂)−2log₅(x)−alpha L(f₁)²+alpha L(f₂)²,

with its continuous extensions at the four endpoints. These are the same scalar and fixed-complement conventions as the earlier endpoint-family theorems. For odd integral a, the away-from-5 heights of P vanish, so h is its global height as well.

**Scalar theorem.** For every odd a divisible by 5, ell is nonzero and

    {z∈C_a(Q₅):rho(z)=0}
       ={(0,1),(0,−1),∞₊,∞₋,(i,0),(−i,0)},  i²=−1.

**Full theorem for a=−605.** Under the previously specified Mordell–Weil-abelian-image and actual-local-image Selmer convention,

    C_−605(Q₅)₂=C_−605(Q)
       ={(0,1),(0,−1),∞₊,∞₋}.

The scalar theorem requires no rank assumption. Its full-depth-two use requires the established rank-one and scalar-containment hypotheses. The full theorem is asserted here only for the already rank-certified parameter −605, using its separately certified nonzero residual period. No uniform nonvanishing assertion for other parameters is made.

## 2. All-order bounds at 5

The earlier endpoint arguments stated some estimates for primes at least seven. The following direct bounds verify what is needed at five; no such range restriction is silently reused.

For a good integral elliptic model with the displayed integral complement, the equation for u=1/X is

    u=t²(1+Au−Au²).

It gives u=t²v(t), v∈1+t²Z₅[[t²]]. The coefficients of ω/dt are integral and even, with constant one. The regular part of −(X−2)ω/dt is integral and even, and its leading term is −t⁻². Integration therefore gives

    v₅([tⁿ](L−t))≥−v₅(n),
    v₅([tⁿ](ζ−1/t))≥−v₅(n)

in the odd degrees where these series occur. The second bound includes degree one. Since B′=ζ(ω/dt)−1/t, for even n≥2 one has

    v₅([tⁿ]B)≥−v₅(n)−floor(log₅(n−1)).               (1)

For even n≥4,

    n−2−v₅(n)−floor(log₅(n−1))≥2.                  (2)

For n=4,6 this is immediate. For even n≥8, use v₅(n)≤n/5 and floor(log₅(n−1))≤(n−1)/5; the resulting upper bound for the losses is at most n−4. For odd n≥3, n−1−v₅(n)≥2. These bounds tend to infinity, so they control the entire evaluated series, not just any truncation. Thus for s=v₅(t)≥1,

    L(t)−t∈5^(3s)Z₅,
    ζ(t)−1/t∈5^sZ₅,
    B(t)∈5^(2s)Z₅.                                 (3)

The same coefficient argument applies on any integral smooth disk. A single integration introduces only its degree denominator. A double integration introduces at worst v₅(n)+floor(log₅(n−1)) in degree n. If the result is even and starts in degree two, (2) shows that its entire higher tail, evaluated at a parameter z∈5Z₅ and divided by z², is integral (indeed is in 25Z₅). For an odd primitive of an integral even differential, its quotient by z equals its linear coefficient modulo 25. These observations will be used both at the rational endpoints and at the Weierstrass points.

## 3. Exact endpoint constants for a divisible by 5

Let s=v₅(a)≥1. It is finite because a is odd. Put

    ψ=a(4−a),  N=a²−8,  Q=3a⁴−20a³+40a²−64.

Exact tripling and the division-polynomial formulas give

    t₃=t(3P)=ψN/Q,   U=ψ₃(P)/t₃=Q/N,
    ell=L(t₃)/3,
    h=(2/9)(log₅(U)−B(t₃)),
    Z=ζ(t₃)/3−8/(3ψ).                              (4)

Both N and Q are units, and t₃/a≡1/2 modulo 5, hence v₅(t₃)=s. The exact identities

    U−8=a²(3a−8)(a−4)/(a²−8),
    1/(3t₃)−8/(3ψ)=−a(3a−8)/(3(a²−8))              (5)

give U≡8 modulo 25 and show that the principal part of Z belongs to 5^sZ₅. Since 8⁴≡21 modulo 25,

    log₅(U)/5≡(8⁴−1)/(4·5)≡1 modulo 5.

All later logarithm terms and B(t₃) have valuation at least two. Equations (3)–(5) therefore prove

    ell/a≡1/6 modulo 5,   v₅(ell)=s,
    h/5≡3 modulo 5,      v₅(h)=1,
    Z∈5^sZ₅,
    v₅(alpha)=1−2s<0.                               (6)

No unit-root or ordinarity assumption occurs here; the fixed integral complement is enough.

For a=−605 the exact rational data are

    ψ=−368445,  N=366017,  Q=406366445311,
    t₃=−134857133565/406366445311,
    U=406366445311/366017,
    Z_principal=−1102915/1098051.

In particular ell/5≡4, h/5≡3, 5alpha≡3 modulo 5, and Z∈5Z₅.

## 4. Endpoint-disk isolation

On either disk centered at (0,ε), ε=±1, write

    y=ε sqrt(1+a x²+a x⁴+x⁶),  x∈5Z₅,
    I=∫₀ˣ t/y(t) dt,  J=∫₀ˣ dt/y(t).

The pullbacks and endpoint values give

    L(f₁)=ε ell+I,  L(f₂)=−J,
    d(ζ∘f₁)=(1−x²)x dx/y,
    d(λ∘f₁)=−2(ζ∘f₁)x dx/y,
    ζ(f₁(0))=ε Z.

Since Z is integral, Section 2 proves

    (λ(f₁)−h)/x²∈Z₅,
    I/x²∈Z₅,  J/x≡ε modulo 5,  I²/x²∈25Z₅.

The other image has t₂=−x(1+x²)/y. Its regularized local-height expression is

    S=−λ(f₂)−2log₅(x)
      =2log₅((1+x²)/y)+2B(t₂).

The unit inside the logarithm is ε times an element of 1+x²Z₅[[x²]]. Its logarithm divided by x² is integral after evaluation; (1)–(3) give the same statement for B(t₂)/x². Hence

    H=((λ(f₁)−h)+S)/x²∈Z₅.

Using h=alpha ell² gives the exact identity, for x≠0,

    rho/(alpha x²)
       =H/alpha+(J/x)²−2ε ell(I/x²)−I²/x²
       ∈1+5Z₅.                                    (7)

Here H/alpha∈5Z₅ by (6), and ell∈5Z₅. Thus rho has no noncentral zero on either finite endpoint disk. Its center is a zero by construction. Reciprocal antisymmetry under (x,y)↦(1/x,y/x³) gives the identical assertion on the two infinity disks.

## 5. Weierstrass-disk isolation

Let r=±i and W=(r,0). The disk is parameterized by y∈5Z₅ and an integral even series x=x(y), with x(0)=r. Indeed f′(r)=−2rA is a unit. Both elliptic images have center T=(0,0), a nonzero two-torsion point on the shifted elliptic equation. Their logarithms and odd primitives vanish at the center.

The pullbacks are

    f₁*ω=x dx/y,  f₂*ω=−dx/y,
    dx/y=2dy/f′(x(y)).

They are integral even differentials in y, with constant coefficients −1/A and −r/A respectively. By the single-integration bound of Section 2,

    L(f₁)/y≡−1/A modulo 5,
    L(f₂)/y≡−r/A modulo 5.                          (8)

The pullbacks of dζ are integral even differentials as well. Starting with ζ(T)=0, integrating dλ=−2ζω and applying the double-integration bound proves that each local-height variation divided by y² is integral. Also log₅(x/r)/y² is integral. Since both quotient images are the same two-torsion point, their heights cancel and their logarithms vanish, so the center value is rho(W)=−2log₅(r)=0.

It follows that for y≠0 there is H_W(y)∈Z₅ with

    rho/(alpha y²)
       =H_W(y)/alpha−(L(f₁)/y)²+(L(f₂)/y)²
       ≡−2/A² modulo 5.                            (9)

The right side is a unit. Thus the only zero in each Weierstrass disk is its center. For a=−605, this additionally gives 5rho/y²≡1 modulo 5 throughout each punctured W-disk.

## 6. Exhaustion of all disks

The reduction at 5 is smooth: (a−3)(a+1) is a unit. Modulo 5 its sextic is x⁶+1. At x=0 it has the two y-values ±1; at x=±1 the value is 2, a nonsquare; at x=±2 the value is zero, with a simple root. The monic degree-six projective model also has exactly the two rational points at infinity. Thus

    C_a(F₅)={(0,±1),(±2,0),∞₊,∞₋}.

Good reduction identifies C_a(Q₅) with the union of these six residue disks. The two nonendpoint centers lift to the exact points (±i,0). Sections 4–5 prove the scalar theorem on every disk.

## 7. Completion of the full-set classification at a=−605

The previously audited rank pattern has a=3−32·19 and 8·19−1=151, both primes with 19≡3 modulo 8; it proves rank E_−605(Q)=1. Also a≡3 modulo 8, so the established first actual away-from-5 target is {0}. The endpoint-basepoint bridge and actual-target containment therefore put the full restricted depth-two set inside the six-point scalar set of Section 1.

The curve is geometrically non-CM because its rational j-invariant has negative valuation at 151. Its actual residual targets all vanish: the dyadic cohomology theorem applies since v₂(a−3)=5, the only positive odd divisor of a−3 is 19≡3 modulo 4, and the remaining primes have zero actual residual image. The exact joint-gluing theorem consequently reduces membership of W± to δ₅(W±)=0.

The independently audited certificate in [the certified residual period](five_adic_residual_certificate.md) proves

    (1/i)∫_(−i,0)^(i,0) (dx/(2y))(x dx/(2y))
        =21625+O(5⁷),

of valuation three. The holomorphic residual criterion gives δ₅(W±)≠0. Both extra scalar zeros are therefore excluded from full depth two. The four remaining points are rational and satisfy the global Selmer conditions. This proves exactly

    C_−605(Q₅)₂=C_−605(Q)
      ={(0,1),(0,−1),∞₊,∞₋}.

This final equality uses scalar necessity, whole-disk isolation, and an independently certified non-scalar obstruction. It does not infer full membership from either scalar equation alone.

## 8. A uniform local period and a conditional full-equality family

The certified period also transfers to an explicit parameter neighborhood. For every integral

    a≡−605 modulo 5³⁰,

put ν_j=x^j dx/(2y) on C_a. Then, with the same orientation and word convention,

    X_a=(1/i)∫_(−i,0)^(i,0) ν₀ν₁
        ≡21625 modulo 5⁷.                           (10)

In particular v₅(X_a)=3. This is a local period statement and requires no rank assumption.

To prove it, carry out the certified regular-Frobenius construction with f_a=x⁶+a x⁴+a x²+1. Its finite free Hensel quotient Z₅[x]/(f_a⁵), the initial root x⁵ modulo 5, the Newton unit inverse, and the resulting polynomial H agree with their a=−605 counterparts modulo 5³⁰. The divisions producing U=f_a(H)/f_a⁵ and P=H′/f_a⁴ are monic; all coefficients therefore continue to agree modulo 5³⁰. The twenty-term binomial polynomials, scaled horizontal-reduction congruences, and normalized endpoint functional likewise give identical finite certificates.

The theoretical degree and denominator bounds remain

    D*=2868,  L*=4,  J*=5.

They depend only on integrality, the fixed degrees, and f_a mod5=x⁶+1. The complete uniform infinite-tail bounds are exactly those of the residual certificate: finite reduction error at least 5²², finite right-hand-side error at least 5¹³, matrix tail at least 5¹⁷, and right-hand-side tail at least 5⁷. The same integral matrix and unit system determinant modulo 5 give the same certified result (10). This is coefficientwise congruence plus an error bound, not an inference from numerical continuity or stabilization.

Consequently suppose in addition that

- a≡3 modulo 16;
- E_a is geometrically non-CM and has rational rank one;
- every odd prime q dividing a−3 satisfies q≡3 modulo 4.

Then the dyadic theorem and odd bad-prime image theorem give the zero residual target. The scalar theorem in Section 1 gives exactly six zeros, and (10) removes the two Weierstrass centers. Under the same restricted Selmer convention,

    C_a(Q₅)₂=C_a(Q)={(0,1),(0,−1),∞₊,∞₋}.           (11)

For a concrete conditional arithmetic subfamily, take

    a=3−32q,  q prime,  q≡19 modulo 4·5³⁰,

and retain the separate assumption rank E_a(Q)=1. Then v₂(a−3)=5 and q≡3 modulo 4, so all actual residual images vanish. Non-CM is automatic here: a+1=−4(8q−1), and every odd prime divisor of 8q−1 has negative valuation in j(E_a)=256a³/(a+1), since gcd(a,a+1)=1. Thus (11) holds whenever that rank-one condition holds. The already certified q=19 recovers a=−605. No infinitude of rank-one members, or of full-equality examples, is claimed.
