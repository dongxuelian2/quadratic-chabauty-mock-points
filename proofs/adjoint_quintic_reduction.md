# A degree-ten ray-class reduction for the actual adjoint example

Put a=−157469, E:Y²=X³+(a−3)X²−(a−3)X and
M=ad⁰(E[5]). Let S={2,5,7,19,37,39367}, together with the infinite place,
and let G_{Q,S} denote the Galois group unramified outside S.
Write χ=χ₅ for the quadratic character of Q(√5), not χ₋₄.

**Theorem.** Let F be the quintic field defined by

    f(t)=t⁵−1574680t²−123981642460t−15618612205226992,

and L=F(√5). There is an F₅-linear isomorphism

    H¹(G_{Q,S},M) ≅ H¹(G_{F,S_F},χ)
                  ≅ Hom(Cl_m(L)/5,F₅)⁻,                 (1)

where m=(5³·2·7·19·37·39367)O_L and the minus sign denotes the
eigenspace for the nontrivial automorphism τ of L/F.
In particular, the unrestricted S-ramified adjoint calculation can use
a degree-ten field instead of the degree-120 projective splitting field.

This is a cohomological reduction, not a computed dimension. It does not
identify the propagated finite/crystalline local conditions with all of
the ray-class space. It does not evaluate δ₅(W) or Θ₅(e₃₇).

The subsequent [local lifting theorem](adjoint_local_lifting.md) reduces
this sufficient modulus to P5^13 Q19 tau(Q19), and identifies the local
zero condition at 19 with removal of the two Q19 factors. Every integral
lift satisfies that necessary condition. The long class-group attempts
are stopped; no ray dimension has been obtained.

## 1. The five-point module

The [exact Frobenius calculation](adjoint_transgression_repair.md) proves
that the projective image on E[5] is G=PGL₂(F₅)≅S₅. Its sign character is
χ, because the determinant modulo squares is the quadratic cyclotomic
character. Let W=F₅⁵ be the five-point permutation module, W₀ the kernel
of the sum map, and 1=(1,1,1,1,1). In characteristic five, 1∈W₀.
There is an isomorphism

    M ≅ (W₀/F₅·1)⊗χ.                                 (2)

Here is an explicit verification, fixing all twists. Take

    u=[1,1;0,1],  v=[0,1;3,0],

whose permutations are (01234) and (01). In the adjoint basis
e=[0,1;0,0], h=diag(1,−1), f=[0,0;1,0], their matrices are

    Ad(u)=[1,3,4;0,1,1;0,0,1],
    Ad(v)=[0,0,2;0,4,0;3,0,0].

In W₀/F₅·1 use d₀,d₁,d₂, where d_i=e_i−e₄ and
d₃=−d₀−d₁−d₂. After the sign twist their matrices are

    D(u)=[4,4,3;1,0,4;0,1,4],
    D(v)=[0,4,0;4,0,0;0,0,4].

The matrix K=[2,1,3;3,3,0;2,4,1] has determinant1 modulo5 and
Ad(u)K=KD(u), Ad(v)K=KD(v). The two generators generate all120
projective matrices, proving (2). The checker verifies this for every
element, including compatibility of the permutation multiplication.

Set A=W₀⊗χ. We have exact sequences of G_{Q,S}-modules

    0 → A → W⊗χ → χ → 0,
    0 → χ → A → M → 0.                              (3)

Unlike an invalid omission of the transgression for the full splitting
field, these are exact sequences of the original Galois modules.

## 2. The auxiliary character has no global H¹ or H²

For this particular S,

    H⁰(G_{Q,S},χ)=H¹(G_{Q,S},χ)=H²(G_{Q,S},χ)=0.      (4)

To prove the positive-degree statements, take K₀=Q(ζ₅), with group
Δ of order4 and cyclotomic character ω:Δ→F₅*. The index is prime to5,
so restriction and averaging identify the desired cohomology with the
ω³-eigenspace of Hⁱ(G_{K₀,S₀},μ₅): indeed χ=ω²=μ₅⊗ω.

The class number of K₀ is1. Its discriminant is125, and the Minkowski
ideal-class bound (4/π)²·4!/4⁴·√125 is less than2. Every ideal class
therefore contains the unit ideal. Its S-class group is also zero.
The Kummer sequence identifies H¹ with S-units modulo fifth powers.
Ordinary units modulo torsion have trivial complex conjugation: their
rank is1 and comes from the maximal real subfield. The fifth roots of
unity have character ω, not ω³. Thus ordinary units contribute no ω³.

The quotient of S-units by ordinary units is the permutation lattice
on primes in S₀, since the S-class group is zero. The prime above5 is
fixed by Δ. For q∈S\{5}, the decomposition group is generated by
q modulo5, and none of these q is1 modulo5. The faithful character ω³
is nontrivial on each such decomposition group, so it does not occur
in any of these prime permutation modules. Semisimplicity for Δ over
F₅ now proves the H¹ assertion.

For H², Kummer theory over the S-integers gives the exact sequence

    0 → Pic(O_{K₀,S₀})/5 → H²(G_{K₀,S₀},μ₅)
      → Br(O_{K₀,S₀})[5] → 0.

Here S includes primes above5, so the usual S-integer/Galois cohomology
identification applies. The Picard term is zero. Local invariants embed
the Brauer term into the direct sum of F₅ over the finite S₀-primes;
the Δ-action is the same permutation action, with no cyclotomic twist
on local Brauer invariants. The preceding decomposition-group argument
again excludes ω³. This proves H²=0. Finally H⁰=0 because χ is nontrivial.

## 3. The quintic is the correct projective field

Fernández–Lario, *On elliptic Galois representations and genus-zero
modular units*, J. Théor. Nombres Bordeaux19 (2007), Proposition4.4 and
Section4.2, give the five-point modular resolvent

    Φ_j(X)=X⁵+(40/j)X²−(5/j)X+1/j.

Its specialization at the j-invariant of an elliptic curve with
surjective projective mod5 representation has that projective splitting
field. Here j=256a³/(a+1), and replacing X by t/(4a) gives exactly

    f(t)=t⁵+10(a+1)t²−5a(a+1)t+4a²(a+1).

Our j is neither0 nor1728, and the projective image is S₅. Thus f is
irreducible, F is the fixed field of a point stabilizer S₄, and W is
Ind_{G_F}^{G_Q}F₅. This identification uses the modular theorem, not
agreement of finitely many Frobenius factorizations. The polynomial
discriminant identity is

    disc(f)=2⁴·5⁵·a²(a+1)⁴(a−3)²(2a+3)⁴.

The field, as a subfield of the E[5] splitting field, is unramified
outside S. The extra polynomial-discriminant factors can be index
factors and do not enlarge S.

Taking cohomology in (3) and using (4) gives isomorphisms

    H¹(G_{Q,S},M) ← H¹(G_{Q,S},A) → H¹(G_{Q,S},W⊗χ).

Shapiro identifies the last group with H¹(G_{F,S_F},χ), proving the
first isomorphism in (1). F cannot contain a quadratic field, so [L:F]=2
and [L:Q]=10. Restriction from F to L, followed by averaging, identifies
H¹(G_{F,S_F},χ) with the anti-invariant continuous additive characters
G_{L,S_L}→F₅.

## 4. A sufficient finite conductor

At primes of L away from5, a degree-five cyclic extension is at most
tamely ramified, and its conductor exponent is at most1. At a prime
P above5, write e=v_P(5). The logarithm identifies U^n with P^n for
n>e/4. Fifth powering then sends U^n onto U^{n+e}. Thus U^{3e} is
contained in the fifth powers of L_P*, since 2e>e/4. Every character
to F₅ kills these fifth powers, giving conductor exponent at most3e.
There is no ramification at infinity in an odd-degree cyclic extension.

Consequently the integer modulus m in (1) contains the conductor of
every S-ramified F₅-valued character. Global class field theory now
identifies the character group with Hom(Cl_m(L)/5,F₅). The modulus is
τ-invariant, so this identification respects the minus eigenspaces.
This proves the theorem.

## 5. Exact field arithmetic and the remaining computation

A smaller defining polynomial for F is

    g(x)=x⁵−1574680x²+2362020x+198368738320.

If x is its root, then

    t=(4x⁴+2x³+x²−5038975x+5668848)/2519489

is a root of f. The checker verifies this identity exactly and that
1,t,t²,t³,t⁴ form a basis in Q[x]/g, proving equality of the fields.
The PARI maximal-order computation gives

    disc(F)=2⁸·5⁵·7²·19²·37²·39367⁴,
    disc(L)=2¹⁶·5¹¹·7⁴·19⁴·37⁴·39367⁸.

These maximal-order calculations do not require a class-group or GRH
assumption. `scripts/compute_adjoint_ray.gp` reproduces them, then
attempts the class group, ray quotient and involution matrix. Class-group
output is conditional until `bnfcertify` returns1; no uncertified
dimension is included in the theorem.

The PARI script now reduces the degree-ten defining polynomial and
transports the involution through an exactly checked field isomorphism.
It checks that the transported map fixes the quintic generator and
negates sqrt(5). Thread count, factor-base size and relation-search
parameters are configurable. Completed class groups have versioned
binary checkpoints; the loader accepts both a single record and PARI's
append-only sequence of records, selecting the last record and rejecting
incompatible field or involution data.

The local PARI attempts exhausted both a 6 GB and a 12 GB main stack
during class-group construction. Smaller-factor-base random and
small-norm searches had insufficient relation yield. No attempt
produced a certified class group or ray dimension. Intermediate
relation counts and tentative class numbers are not class-group results.

`scripts/compute_adjoint_ray.jl` provides an independent Hecke/Nemo route,
tested with Hecke 0.41.1 and Julia 1.10.13. It constructs L from

    A(x)=-x⁵−50x³−1574680x²−2362145x+198360864920,
    B(x)=5x⁴+50x²+3149360x+2362045,
    A(x)²−5B(x)²=0.

For its root alpha, gamma=-A(alpha)/B(alpha) satisfies gamma²=5,
and beta=gamma−alpha satisfies g(beta)=0. The map
alpha↦alpha−2gamma therefore fixes beta and negates gamma. These
identities and the maximal-order discriminant are checked exactly.
Hecke collects sparse relations using the order-two automorphism and
computes the kernel of tau+1 on the exponent-five ray quotient. The
same ray-action routine was independently exercised on Q(sqrt(5)),
where unconditional computation returns a zero-dimensional minus part
for this modulus.

The default full calculation first prints explicitly conditional class
and ray data, then requests `ray_class_group(...; GRH=false)`, which
certifies both class and unit groups before recomputing the ray quotient.
The equality of the two minus dimensions is checked. A conditional
result is never relabeled certified merely because it is unchanged.
No degree-ten dimension is asserted here.

From a Julia environment containing Hecke, run

```sh
ADJOINT_FIELD_ONLY=1 julia --project=<Hecke environment> scripts/compute_adjoint_ray.jl
julia --project=<Hecke environment> scripts/compute_adjoint_ray.jl
```

`ADJOINT_FACTOR_BOUND` optionally changes the relation factor base;
`ADJOINT_CONDITIONAL_ONLY=1` explicitly stops before certification.
The first command checks the exact field construction without starting
the expensive class-group stage.

Even a certified value for (1) is not the required finite Selmer group.
The local long exact sequences from (3) have their own H⁰ and H¹ terms;
the global character vanishing (4) cannot be imposed locally. In
particular the crystalline condition at5 must be transported through
these sequences, and the relevant integral lattices must be retained.
The final comparison δ₅(W)∈{0,±Θ₅(e₃₇)} remains unresolved.
