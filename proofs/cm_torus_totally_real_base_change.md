# Exact norm-one torus targets over a totally real field

Let F be totally real, K/F a CM quadratic extension with involution c, and T the norm-one torus of K/F. Fix a rational prime p. Write V for V_pT and let S be a finite set of finite places of F away from p. At all places over p impose the finite Bloch–Kato condition. At finite places outside S and away from p impose the unramified condition.

## An exact test for one rational valuation tuple

Let w be a finitely supported rational divisor on the finite primes of K, supported above S and satisfying c(w)=−w. Use the convention ord_P(π_P)=1. At a split place q in S, choosing one prime of K over q gives the one-dimensional V-valued local Kummer coordinate through the valuation. The other prime has the opposite coordinate. At a nonsplit place the local group is zero: after restriction to K the valuation row is fixed by c, so its minus part is zero. Restriction is injective because the coefficient field has characteristic zero.

Let M/K be a finite number-field extension and t∈M*. Fix a place P of M above the coefficient place v|p of F, and assume t is a unit there. Use the splitting of T over M_P induced by K⊂M to compare the local Kummer class with that of t. The following conditions are equivalent:

1. A class in H¹(F,V) with the above local conditions has away-from-p tuple w and, after restriction to M_P, has local Kummer class κ(t).
2. Every complex embedding σ of M satisfies |σ(t)|=1, and for every finite prime Q of M over a prime q of K one has ord_Q(t)=e(Q/q)w_q.

The matching global class, if it exists, is unique. Consequently, for actual torus targets with rational normalized local valuations, one can test each allowed tuple using only algebraic divisors and complex absolute values. The assertion concerns the central comparison in the detecting quotient. A global class in that quotient can still require a lift to full depth two.

**Proof.** Kummer theory and restriction to K identify the specified global space with the minus part of O_K[S_K⁻¹]* ⊗ Q_p. After restriction, the finite condition above p is the unit condition for Q_p(1). This condition is also detected after restriction, since the restriction map on H¹ with B_cris coefficients is injective by averaging over the finite extension group. Class groups are finite, so the rational valuation map on S-units is onto the rational divisors supported on S_K. Its kernel is the space of ordinary units. The minus part of the ordinary unit space is zero. An integral norm-one unit of K has absolute value one at every complex embedding because σ(c(u)) is the complex conjugate of σ(u). Such a unit is a root of unity by Kronecker's theorem. The minus part therefore has the anti-invariant divisor space as its rational model, and the rational tuple w selects a unique rational point in that model.

Concretely, take a positive integer n and an S-unit u of K of norm one with div(u)=nw. To construct u, take a multiple of w that is integral and principal, with generator b, then take b/c(b) and double the multiplier. The global class is κ(u)/n by the valuation isomorphism. If its restriction matches κ(t), the p-adic unit tⁿ/u has zero Kummer class in H¹(M_P,Q_p(1)). The kernel on local units consists of roots of unity, so tⁿ/u is a root of unity also as an algebraic number. This proves both conditions in 2. Conversely, the conditions in 2 make tⁿ/u an ordinary unit of M of absolute value one at every complex embedding. Kronecker's theorem again makes it a root of unity, giving the required equality of local classes. ∎

For the line-bundle torsors in the [CM proof](rational_cm_weierstrass_classification.md), rationality of the away-from-p coordinates is a consequence of [Betts, *The motivic anabelian geometry of local heights on abelian varieties*, Theorem 1.1.6](https://arxiv.org/html/1706.04850v3). This theorem applies after splitting the torus and translating the abelian basepoint. Its rational normalized valuation differs from the discrete valuation by the corresponding ramification factor. In particular the test extends the absolute-value obstruction over Q to CM quadratic extensions of any totally real base. If S contains no split places, its only possible tuple is zero and the test reduces to t being a root of unity.

## An explicit new visibility case over Q(√3)

**Proposition.** Put F=Q(√3) and C=C_(−3/2). The Jacobian has rank two over F. At every good finite place v|p, every F_v-rational Weierstrass point is excluded from the full depth-two set with the F-rational Mordell–Weil abelian image, actual local conditions away from p, and crystalline conditions at all places above p. At the unique place above 7, all six Weierstrass points are visible, including the pair x=±i that is absent over Q_7.

**Proof of the rank.** Use E_6: y²=x³−36x from the CM proof. Its rank over Q is one. The quadratic twist by 3 is y²=x³−324x, which is isomorphic over Q to E_2: y²=x³−4x by a square scaling of x by 9 and a cube scaling of y by 27. The eigenspace decomposition under Gal(F/Q) identifies the plus part of the rational Mordell–Weil space with E_6(Q)⊗Q and the minus part with the twist's rational space. In the shifted coordinate X=x−2 the curve E_2 has coefficients A=6,B=8 in y²=X³+AX²+BX. The two-isogenous curve has A'=−12,B'=4. In the first descent the possible classes are ±1,±2, all witnessed by the identity and the three rational two-torsion points. In the dual descent the same four classes are possible, but either negative class gives a strictly negative quartic on every primitive pair of integers. For the class 2 the quartic is

    N²=2U⁴−12U²V²+2V⁴.

Opposite parities give right side 2 modulo 4. If both U and V are odd, the right side is 8 modulo 16. Thus the dual group has size one and the two-isogeny rank formula gives 2^rank=4·1/4. This proves rank zero for the twist, hence rank one for E_6 over F. The isogeny Jac(C)→E_6² gives rank two for Jac(C)/F.

**Proof of the exclusions.** For p≥5, base change the detecting quotient and the proper elliptic reference sections from CM proof §§4–5.1 to F. For each of the six points, the torus fiber comparison with its reference has precisely the value already computed in that proof, up to inversion. These values are 1/3 for x=±i and (−1+2√−2)/3 for x=±√2 or ±1/√2. They are units at all primes over p≥5. The first is a nonunit at 3 and the second has nonintegral trace −2/3, so neither is a root of unity. The geometric comparison is unchanged by base change, including the proper reference signs.

At each v|p, the local image of the F-rational abelian Mordell–Weil space in the detecting quotient is injective. Indeed, on each E_6 factor it is generated by the image of one nontorsion point, whose elliptic logarithm at v is nonzero because the local logarithm has torsion kernel. Thus a putative full Selmer lift has the same global abelian class as the proper reference. The resulting central difference is unique, by H⁰(F,V_p(E_6²))=0, and finite at every v|p by crystalline central comparison with the proper reference at those places. The local and global arguments in CM proof §4 apply over a number field unchanged: local torsion is finite, the proper reference paths are crystalline at good places above p, and the exactness of D_cris on crystalline representations identifies the central difference in the finite local fiber. At finite places away from 2,3,p both lifts are unramified, as C and the proper reference curves have good reduction there. Thus S can consist of the primes of F over 2 and 3.

The center is that of K=F(i) over F. Above 2 one has F_v=Q_2(√3), and i does not lie in it because −1, 3 and −3 are all nonsquares in Q_2, as seen on units modulo 8. Above 3 the extension Q_3(√3)/Q_3 is ramified and the residue field is F_3, where −1 is nonsquare. Hence neither place in S splits in K. The eligible global center is zero by the valuation isomorphism above. A local value κ(t) could therefore occur only if t were a root of unity. All six possibilities are excluded. At the places over 2 and 3 there are no visible Weierstrass points. In addition to the nonsplitting of i, the square root of 2 is absent. Both 2 and 2/3 are nonsquares over Q_2 by valuation parity. Over Q_3, 2 is nonsquare modulo 3 and 2/3 has odd valuation.

Finally, 3 is nonsquare modulo 7, so the unique completion of F above 7 is the unramified quadratic extension of Q_7. It contains i, since −1 becomes square in F_49 and the roots lift. Also 2 is already square in Q_7, and the six listed x-coordinates are distinct, with y=0. The sextic model has good reduction at 7. ∎
